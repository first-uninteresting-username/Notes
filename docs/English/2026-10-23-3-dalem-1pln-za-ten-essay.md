Receiving presents is always exciting, but people disagree about whether it is better to ask for something specific or wait for a surprise. Although unexpected gifts can be memorable, I believe that telling others what you would like is usually the better choice.

Firstly, asking for a particular present increases the chance of receiving something useful. Everyone has different interests, and even close friends cannot always guess what we need. For example, a person who enjoys reading may already own the book someone plans to buy. Suggesting a few titles can prevent disappointment and ensure that the gift will be appreciated.

Secondly, clear suggestions can make shopping easier for the giver. Choosing a present can be stressful, especially when time or money is limited. A list containing options at different prices allows someone to choose comfortably. Asking politely also shows that we value their effort, provided we avoid making demands or expecting expensive items.

Of course, surprises have their own charm. An unexpected gift can show how well someone knows us and create a special memory. However, sharing general preferences still leaves room for surprise, since the giver can choose the exact item.

In conclusion, I prefer asking for what I want because it helps both people enjoy the experience. A thoughtful suggestion does not remove the meaning of a present; gratitude and kindness matter most.
